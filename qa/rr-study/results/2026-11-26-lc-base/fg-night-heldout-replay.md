# FINE-GRID held-out replay: the FG ON night's own cycles through the shipped build before FINE-GRID and through today's default

**Date:** 2026-11-28 · **Author:** QA · **Status:** a measurement, no bars; aggregates only (NFR-021: no message text, no callsign; the live cross-check reads the two `ALL.TXT` inside one function and prints counts).
**Why:** the FINE-GRID default (thin 1 + 4 x 4) was chosen from three replay slices (S40, H40, H20). The night `20261128_0038` (live recording of the station on `main` `c6d54afb` with that default) is data the choice never saw, and the live night alone cannot give an A/B (one daemon, one band condition). So: the same archived cycles through both builds, scored against the same WSJT-X.

**Builds and runs (HK-022).** `nx` = the XC-ROUND confirmation build (`libft8.dll` `fa0b96c1c8a220b3...`, shim 20260082, the build before FINE-GRID; harness `bin_xn`); `nf` = today's default (`libft8.dll` **`0ef023d55a381db9...` = pin**, shim 20260083, `--lean-thin 1 --lean-time-osr 4 --lean-freq-osr 4`; harness `bin_fg`). Both: lean, `--lean-threads 16`, `--effort normal`, `--lean-probe 1`, cross-cycle stage and XC round on, the flags of the earlier replays; cycles = the night's archived WAVs in order (2,490, no gap other than 15 s), reference = the night's WSJT-X `ALL.TXT`. Runner `qa/decoder-rebuild/fg_night_run.py` (a wrapper of `lc_base_run.py`), arm.json `bin_pin` asserted in the analysis `fg_night_analyze.py`. Two **contiguous blocks of 400 cycles**, chosen from WSJT-X's rows per cycle **before any decode**: **NB (busy)** `261128_080645` to `261128_094630` (37.4 WSJT-X decodes per cycle), **NQ (quiet)** `261128_005945` to `261128_023930` (15.4 per cycle; the night's average is 26.0). No `--filter` (these are replays). **First check, before any rate was read: PASS for `nf` on both blocks** (shadowed 0, sub-positions 0..3, published decodes on odd sub-positions, XC round rows present).

## Summary

1. **The gain replicates out of sample where the band is busy, and it is zero where the band is quiet.** Busy block: **rate 90.706 % to 92.249 % (+1.543 pp), matched +231** of 14,966 WSJT-X decodes; the paired per-cycle difference in matched decodes is **+0.578 +/- 0.060 (mean / standard error, 9.6 standard errors), 180 cycles better and 46 worse of 400**. Quiet block: **87.025 % to 87.041 % (+0.016 pp), matched +1** of 6,158; the paired difference is +0.003 +/- 0.037 (67 better, 61 worse). The three LC-BASE slices (about 40 decodes per cycle) gave +2.08 / +1.39 / +0.60 pp: the busy block, at 37 per cycle, lies inside that range.
2. **By crowding (WSJT-X decodes per cycle, busy block):** 36 or more (275 cycles) **+1.83 pp**; 26 to 35 (112 cycles) **+1.03 pp**; 16 to 25 (13 cycles) -2.56 pp (13 cycles, 312 reference decodes: too few to read). Quiet block: 0 to 15 (219 cycles) +0.14 pp, 16 to 25 (180 cycles) -0.06 pp. So the gain grows with the number of signals per cycle and is absent below about 25.
3. **Unconfirmed decodes:** `ours_only` +0.29 pp in the busy block (3.889 % to 4.183 %), +0.23 pp in the quiet block (4.125 % to 4.352 %); ours / WSJT-X 94.59 % to 96.43 % and 91.15 % to 91.39 %.
4. **Cost:** CPU per cycle 16.03 to 17.82 CPU-s (+11 %) in the busy block and 6.86 to 7.02 (+2 %) in the quiet block; `last_p95` 2,423 to 2,526 ms and 1,311 to 1,284 ms; 0 abandoned cycles, 0 native errors in all four runs.
5. **The replay reproduces the live daemon.** The live daemon's matched share of the same cycles (the ANOVA's rule: same cycle, same normalised text) against the replay of the default: busy **92.323 % vs 92.249 %** (13,817 vs 13,806 matched), quiet **86.633 % vs 87.041 %** (5,334 vs 5,360). The harness and the daemon agree to 0.07 pp on the busy block and 0.4 pp on the quiet one (the daemon ran live, with the early-decode timing and real-time deadlines the replay does not have).

## What this says (QA's reading; the Captain decides)

The default is supported by data the choice was not made on: **about +1.5 pp when the band is busy (and +1.8 at 36 or more decodes per cycle), nothing when it is quiet, at +11 % CPU when busy and +2 % when quiet, with no loss in the quiet block.** The live night's 90.4 % matched is therefore what the default delivers on a half-busy night, and the earlier nights' lower figures (88.2 % on the XC night) were partly a different build, partly different crowding: this replay does not separate those two for the live nights, but for the same audio it shows the build effect directly. Caveats: two blocks of one night; the crowding bins are small at the edges; the baseline `nx` is the build before FINE-GRID, not the classic decoder.
